# Evidence base

The sources behind the app's numbers, and which numbers are conventions (first collected in r13, kept up to date since). CLAUDE.md's rule: no number without a source here; a convention is labelled as one, here and in a tip. Moved out of CLAUDE.md unchanged in r54.

- **1RM:** Brzycki up to 10 reps to failure, Epley at 11–12 (they agree at 10). Nuzzo 2024 and Halperin 2022 on how estimates spread at higher reps. No correction per exercise: nothing here establishes how a formula errs on a given lift (r48; an earlier comment said deadlifts read low and leg presses high, with no source, and was removed). The number is labelled an estimate wherever it shows (`fmtE`).
- **Hard sets:** RIR ≤ 3 (Robinson 2024, Refalo 2023).
- **The 2026 ACSM position stand (checked against the full text in r48):** Currier et al., Med Sci Sports Exerc 2026;58(4):851-872, an overview of 137 systematic reviews. Hypertrophy was enhanced by higher volumes (≥10 sets a muscle a week) and eccentric overload. Strength was enhanced by loads ≥80% 1RM, a full range of motion, 2–3 sets, the main lifts first in the session and ≥2 sessions a week. Training to failure, equipment type, set structure, time under tension and periodization did not consistently change outcomes. The two-sessions-a-week finding is about strength, not growth: with weekly volume matched, frequency has no detectable effect on hypertrophy (Schoenfeld, Grgic and Krieger 2019, J Sports Sci 37(11):1286-1295, 25 studies; Pelland 2025 found frequency's effect on hypertrophy compatible with none and on strength positive with diminishing returns), and a split and a full-body routine grow muscle and strength the same (Ramos-Campo 2024, J Strength Cond Res 38(7):1330-1340, 14 studies). So the weekly bands (10–20) and the per-session flag carry the evidence; which days a muscle lands on does not, and no routine is called better than another for growth.
- **Helper muscles (Pelland 2025, Sports Med 56(2):481-505):** of the three ways to count a set that trains a muscle indirectly (1, 0.5, 0), 0.5 fitted the dose-response data best, which is why helpers count half. Which muscles the library lists as helpers on each lift is the library's own call, a convention: the paper classified sets as direct or indirect for the muscle it measured and says nothing about grip or holding muscles, so the forearms and traps on a deadlift, or the lower back on a row, count half here by the same rule with no study behind that choice. Said in the helper tip and the Guide (r48).
- **Rest between sets (Singer 2024, Front Sports Act Living, a Bayesian meta-analysis of 9 studies, mostly untrained young men):** a small benefit to growth from resting more than 60 s, and no difference detected past 90 s. The default plan rests 90 s, Strength 3 minutes (Schoenfeld 2016); the ready-made routines rest 45 to 60 s on many isolation lifts, which sits at the short edge of that finding (PENDING 302, V's call).
- **Target RIR of 1 (a convention, r29.5):** hypertrophy improves as sets end closer to failure (Robinson 2024, Sports Medicine meta-regressions), but training to failure grew the quadriceps no more than stopping at 1 to 2 RIR in trained lifters, with more velocity and rep loss (Refalo 2024, J Sports Sci). 1 sits between; templates use 0 on some isolation lifts and 2 on some heavy or bodyweight lifts. Helper muscles count 0.5 (Pelland 2025).
- **Blank RIR (a convention, r20):** RIR is optional. A blank is read as the lift's usual RIR, the median of its rated working sets in the last 12 weeks once there are 3 (`rirUsual`, `rirEst`), for e1RM, PRs, stalls and hard sets; with fewer, it counts as hard and as 0 for estimates (errs low). Recovery, calibration and the light-load recalibration read only entered RIR. People under-call reps left by about 1 on average (Halperin 2022), so a called 0 is kept as a call.
- **Weekly and per-session volume:** major-muscle bands of 10–20 are typical, not a ceiling. The per-session flag sits above 11 sets (Remmert 2025, preprint).
- **Deloads** round down to a loadable step, so they never land back on the full load: whole steps of the exercise's own step, never under the bar on a compound barbell lift typed as the total (r27; curls on EZ or fixed bars and per-side numbers have no floor).
- **Well past the range (a convention, r27):** uses the same e1RM estimate and 10% cap (`jumpCap`) as the RIR recalibration; no new number.
- **Moving and stalls:** "moving" needs ≥ 3% and a slope whose two-sided 80% interval (Student's t, n−2 degrees of freedom; `t90`) is above zero; estimated 1RM varies 2–8% day to day (Sigvaldsen 2023). Muscle trends count only lifts that are moving or falling. A stall is a flat or falling slope over the last 6 sessions, widened to cover ≥ 21 days when 6 do not (at least 4 sessions).
- **Recovery tiers:** 1, 2 or 3 days by set count, +1 day after failure sets or a new lift, +0.5 after heavy hinges, Nordics or walking lunges, −1 if every set stopped at RIR ≥ 3. This is a rule of thumb, used only for ranking.
- **Training blocks and trends (conventions, r30):** a session is read as lighter at 10% or more under the load of its previous three and 7% or more under their reps-alone reading (a deload is usually about 10% off; day-to-day swings in estimated strength are 2 to 8%, Sigvaldsen 2023). RIR enters a trend only when rated on 80% or more of a lift's sets. Trends read up to 20 reps to failure with Epley (formulas spread past 12, so never shown as a 1RM, but one lift against itself at similar reps still shows direction). A rep PR counts at 90% or more of the strongest set before it. A lift in a week read as lighter is read lighter too when its load is under its usual or its reading more than 3% under (97%, the r30 audit's tolerance); since r53 the same 97% (`USUAL_SHARE`) decides whether a lift on a lighter or deload day was at its usual level, on the reading, and, a stricter convention of r53's, on the reps a set for a lift with no reading. An exercise with fewer than half its sets done is trimmed. A harder bodyweight swap (`HARD_EX`) gets 3 to 6 reps. None of these moves a number in the evidence above.
- **Tracked-only muscles (a convention, r29):** serratus, rotator cuff, neck and tibialis (`TRACK_ONLY`) are counted and shown with no target: no study sets a weekly dose for them, and most routines leave them to indirect work.
- **First-run targets (a convention, r29):** picking a ready-made routine on a first run lowers each weekly minimum to what it plans, only while every target is still the default and never raising anything; targets the lifter set are left alone. It moves no number in the evidence; it says the plan is the lifter's target, and the toast says so.
- **Second review conventions (r31):** the working load is the load done for the most sets among loads at 60% or more of the session's heaviest; flat is under 0.1% a week; a new level is lighter sessions in a row over 2 weeks or more; a trend needs 3 sessions over 2 weeks; load-finding reads sets past 12 reps to failure with Epley up to 30 (never shown) and allows a 25% jump in a lift's first 3 sessions; only compound lifts with an estimate count toward a deload suggestion; a routine made from an import uses the common ranges nearest the usual reps (1-3, 3-5, 6-8, 8-10, 10-12, 12-15, 15-20). None moves a number in the evidence above.
- **Third review conventions (r32):** a Lighter day or deload is 90% of the normal day's load (or last time's, if lower); a step is large when the estimate says it costs 1.5 reps or more, and then reps build first, up to a quarter of the top of the range past it; one set under the range among the others counts as the bottom of the range; four sessions in a row under the range at one load drop it; a fixed-rep plan (range under 3 reps wide) steps up whatever the step costs; a stall's slope is the median of pairwise slopes; a muscle's trend counts lifts with 4 sessions over 3 weeks; a PR is counted when the set is stronger than every earlier one by the rep-PR score; a dumbbell or kettlebell step is the largest common rack spacing the logged loads fit. None moves a number in the evidence above.
- **RIR 4 to 5 as half a set (a convention, r40, a setting off by default):** growth rises as sets end closer to failure (Robinson 2024), so sets further away still do something; half is a convention, not a measured dose. It moves weekly sets and targets only.
- **Lighter weeks and the first week (conventions, r53 and r54):** a session is lighter when half or more of its lifts were done lighter, and a week when more than half its sessions were or it ends with two or more deload sessions done lighter; a rest week and a week with nothing trained count with them for holding a deload suggestion back. The log's first week is left out of the weekly averages, since loads are found that week with sets ended well short of failure. Neither moves a number in the evidence above; they decide which weeks the averages read.
- **Sessions a week on a cycle (V's call, r54):** a cycle that is not a week long plans the whole number of sessions under its rate (every other day, 3.5, is 3), at least one, for the ring, the streak and adherence. A convention about counting, not training.
- **One rule for every session (V's rule, r54; conventions in its numbers):** a lift sets its next target only at its usual load and reps for its usual sets. Its usual is the middle of this session and the two before it in the range since its last break, leaving out sessions done lighter (by sets, working load and strength reading; before only, for a session labelled lighter or a deload); its usual sets are never more than the plan asks. A session's sets are its working sets within a tenth of its working load either way (r30's lighter threshold) and any heavier one at its usual working load, so a last set tried a step up, or the rest lowered after a hard first set, count, and a back-off set a tenth lighter does not (from the independent review of r54); a session is compared with its usual over as many of its sets at the working load as the usual sets, so a set past them never counts against it. A session with fewer sets, or a reading under 97% of its usual (r30's tolerance), or with no reading a lighter working load or under 97% of its usual reps a set, is short of it: the target repeats, and the trend, stalls and PRs still read it. A break is more than 28 days between two sessions of a lift, the back-after-a-break number below; it restarts the lift's usual, its lighter reading, its trend and its stall. None moves a number in the evidence above: they decide which sessions set targets.
- **A change of units (a convention, r54):** a load typed in one unit is read as an earlier load typed in the other when it lies above that load by no more than half the largest spacing loads come in that its own number is a whole multiple of (5 or 2.5 lb; 2.5, 2, 1 or 0.5 kg): the nearest load over it on that spacing. Only ever lighter, so it takes away a PR made of rounding and makes none. A spacing finer than the load shows (70 kg on 1 kg plates) can read one step past the nearest as the same load. It decides only which sets are PRs.
- **r55 conventions (V's answers of 2026-10-19 and the journey audit of r54):** a set ticked with 0 reps is a missed set, kept and counted toward nothing (V's call); since r56.2 the next target reads one at the working load as a set short of the range, so sets missed never step a load up (the audit: a 5 x 5 with two sets missed twice asked for the next load). A load asks before it is logged when it is more than three times, or under a third of, the heaviest set ticked in the session, or more than twice the lift's heaviest logged (`ODD_SESSION`, `ODD_BEST`; V chose to ask, the numbers are conventions set to catch a slipped digit or the other unit: an unmarked ramp of warm-ups climbs less than threefold a set, a back-off set is rarely under half the top set, and no lift doubles its best in a session). A rep target counts a set past the top of the range as the top, so it never asks for reps the range does not hold. A timed hold back after more than 28 days holds about 90% as long (`LIGHT_SHARE`, the same 90% as a loaded lift's, r34.2). The weeks counted (averages, adherence, the coach's missed weeks) start after a break of more than 28 days, as a lift's readings do (`BACK_DAYS`, Bosquet 2013, below). A lift in more than one rep range reads falling only when it falls in each (V, PENDING 319). None moves a number in the evidence above.
- **Bodyweight share:** only the push-up figure is measured (64%, Ebben 2011); the rest are segment-mass estimates.
- **Numbers with no study behind them (conventions, labelled in r56.2 from the audit):** a drop set counts half a set (`setCredit`); + Drop starts at about 75% of the set before it; the warm-up ramp is 40, 60, 75 and 85% of the working load; at Finish an unmarked lighter set before the first top set is read as a warm-up when it was logged at 5+ RIR, or with no RIR 5+ reps short of what the best estimate allows at that load, or with neither under 75% of the top load (80% on isolation work); the weekly minimums of the smaller muscles (traps, forearms, obliques, abductors and the like) are set by eye from the major-muscle bands, not by a study; PRs are judged in bands of reps to failure of 1 to 5, 6 to 10 and 11 to 12 (`rtfBand`); the coach reads sessions made a week, rounded to a whole session, against the sessions the plan has before it reads how sets were done (r56.2).
- **Back after a break (a convention, r34.2):** a lift more than 28 days off starts at about 90% of its last working load with 2 or more reps in reserve: strength is largely kept for up to about 4 weeks without training and falls faster after (Bosquet 2013, a meta-analysis of training cessation). 90% is the same lighter-day number as r32, not a new one. Every set at 5 or more RIR steps up at least once, since 5 is the top of the scale and only a floor for the estimate.
- **Your pace (a convention, r43):** the time model (40 s a set and so on) is itself a convention; once 3 sessions are timed, estimates scale by the median of how long yours took against it, over the newest 8 in 12 weeks, leaving out a third or three times the model as a clock left on or stopped. It moves minutes only, never a load, a rep or a set.
- **Your training (r44):** heavier loads gave more strength for similar growth (Schoenfeld 2017, meta-analysis of low vs high loads; ACSM 2009 position stand, 1 to 6 RM for strength past the novice stage, 8 to 12 RM for novices), and 3 minutes' rest beat 1 for strength and size (Schoenfeld 2016). 5 to 8 for Both, 2 in reserve on heavy compounds, and 3 or 2 in reserve for a first year are conventions. Focus muscles: more weekly sets grow a muscle more (Pelland 2025); 30% up is a convention; the rest at a third of the minimum is maintenance (Bickel 2011; Spiering 2021 review). The strength focus's 5 days and 3% are conventions (3% is the app's own threshold for a real change).
- **Waist-to-height:** 0.5 / 0.6 bands (NICE 2022).
- **Tape measurements:** changes under about 1 cm are treated as noise.
- **Long-length bonus:** only for exercises backed by trials (seated leg curl, overhead triceps, standing calf raise) or by mechanics (labelled as such).
