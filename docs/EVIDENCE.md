# Evidence base

The sources behind the app's numbers, and which numbers are conventions (first collected in r13, kept up to date since). CLAUDE.md's rule: no number without a source here; a convention is labelled as one, here and in a tip. Moved out of CLAUDE.md unchanged in r54.

- **1RM:** Brzycki up to 10 reps to failure, Epley at 11–12 (they agree at 10). Nuzzo 2024 and Halperin 2022 on how estimates spread at higher reps. No correction per exercise: nothing here establishes how a formula errs on a given lift (r48; an earlier comment said deadlifts read low and leg presses high, with no source, and was removed). The number is labelled an estimate wherever it shows (`fmtE`).
- **Hard sets:** RIR ≤ 3 (Robinson 2024, Refalo 2023).
- **The 2026 ACSM position stand (checked against the full text in r48):** Currier et al., Med Sci Sports Exerc 2026;58(4):851-872, an overview of 137 systematic reviews. Hypertrophy was enhanced by higher volumes (≥10 sets a muscle a week) and eccentric overload. Strength was enhanced by loads ≥80% 1RM, a full range of motion, 2–3 sets, the main lifts first in the session and ≥2 sessions a week. Training to failure, equipment type, set structure, time under tension and periodization did not consistently change outcomes. The two-sessions-a-week finding is about strength, not growth: with weekly volume matched, frequency has no detectable effect on hypertrophy (Schoenfeld, Grgic and Krieger 2019, J Sports Sci 37(11):1286-1295, 25 studies; Pelland 2025 found frequency's effect on hypertrophy compatible with none and on strength positive with diminishing returns), and a split and a full-body routine grow muscle and strength the same (Ramos-Campo 2024, J Strength Cond Res 38(7):1330-1340, 14 studies). So the weekly bands (10–20) and the per-session flag carry the evidence; which days a muscle lands on does not, and no routine is called better than another for growth.
- **Helper muscles (Pelland 2025, Sports Med 56(2):481-505):** of the three ways to count a set that trains a muscle indirectly (1, 0.5, 0), 0.5 fitted the dose-response data best, which is why helpers count half. Which muscles the library lists as helpers on each lift is the library's own call, a convention: the paper classified sets as direct or indirect for the muscle it measured and says nothing about grip or holding muscles, so the forearms and traps on a deadlift, or the lower back on a row, count half here by the same rule with no study behind that choice. Said in the helper tip and the Guide (r48).
- **Rest between sets (Singer 2024, Front Sports Act Living, a Bayesian meta-analysis of 9 studies, mostly untrained young men):** a small benefit to growth from resting more than 60 s, and no difference detected past 90 s. The default plan rests 90 s, Strength 3 minutes (Schoenfeld 2016); the ready-made routines rest an isolation lift 90 s at least since r64 (`ISO_REST`; they rested 45 to 60 s on many up to r63, at the short edge of that finding; PENDING 302, V's call of 2026-10-25).
- **Target RIR of 1 (a convention, r29.5):** hypertrophy improves as sets end closer to failure (Robinson 2024, Sports Medicine meta-regressions), but training to failure grew the quadriceps no more than stopping at 1 to 2 RIR in trained lifters, with more velocity and rep loss (Refalo 2024, J Sports Sci). 1 sits between; templates use 0 on some isolation lifts and 2 on some heavy or bodyweight lifts. Helper muscles count 0.5 (Pelland 2025).
- **Blank RIR (a convention, r20):** RIR is optional. A blank is read as the lift's usual RIR, the median of its rated working sets in the last 12 weeks once there are 3 (`rirUsual`, `rirEst`), for e1RM, PRs, stalls and hard sets; with fewer, it counts as hard and as 0 for estimates (errs low). Recovery, calibration and the light-load recalibration read only entered RIR. People under-call reps left by about 1 on average (Halperin 2022), so a called 0 is kept as a call. Reading a blank as 0 instead (PENDING 339 o, reconsidered and kept as is in r58) would make PRs depend on tapping: 100 x 8 left blank would read as to failure, and the same 100 x 8 rated 2 a week later as a new best. The usual reading makes rating irrelevant for a set at the lifter's usual effort; its one weak spot is a blank set taken much harder than usual, which reads a few percent high.
- **Weekly and per-session volume:** major-muscle bands of 10–20 are typical, not a ceiling. The per-session flag sits above 11 sets (Remmert 2025, preprint).
- **Deloads** round down to a loadable step, so they never land back on the full load: whole steps of the exercise's own step, never under the bar on a compound barbell lift typed as the total (r27; curls on EZ or fixed bars and per-side numbers have no floor), and never above last time's load: where nothing lighter is loadable (20 kg on the bar, read in lb beside the 45 lb bar the app assumes), last time's own load (r64).
- **Well past the range (a convention, r27):** uses the same e1RM estimate and 10% cap (`jumpCap`) as the RIR recalibration; no new number.
- **Moving and stalls:** "moving" needs ≥ 3% and a slope whose two-sided 80% interval (Student's t, n−2 degrees of freedom; `t90`) is above zero; estimated 1RM varies 2–8% day to day (Sigvaldsen 2023). Muscle trends count only lifts that are moving or falling. A stall is a flat or falling slope over the last 6 sessions, widened to cover ≥ 21 days when 6 do not (at least 4 sessions).
- **Recovery tiers:** 1, 2 or 3 days by set count, +1 day after failure sets or a new lift, +0.5 after heavy hinges, Nordics or walking lunges, −1 if every set stopped at RIR ≥ 3. This is a rule of thumb, used only for ranking.
- **Training blocks and trends (conventions, r30):** a session is read as lighter at 10% or more under the load of its previous three and 7% or more under their reps-alone reading (a deload is usually about 10% off; day-to-day swings in estimated strength are 2 to 8%, Sigvaldsen 2023). RIR enters a trend only when rated on 80% or more of a lift's sets. Trends read up to 20 reps to failure with Epley (formulas spread past 12, so never shown as a 1RM, but one lift against itself at similar reps still shows direction). A rep PR counts at 90% or more of the strongest set before it. A lift in a week read as lighter is read lighter too when its load is under its usual or its reading more than 3% under (97%, the r30 audit's tolerance); since r53 the same 97% (`USUAL_SHARE`) decides whether a lift on a lighter or deload day was at its usual level, on the reading, and, a stricter convention of r53's, on the reps a set for a lift with no reading. An exercise with fewer than half its sets done is trimmed. A harder bodyweight swap (`HARD_EX`) gets 3 to 6 reps. None of these moves a number in the evidence above.
- **Which muscles have a target (339 e, V 2026-10-24, r58; a convention):** the muscles lifters train directly for size (`TARGETED`: chest, lats, upper back, side delts, rear delts, biceps, triceps, quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves, abs) have a default weekly target, since the dose-response research measures such muscles (Pelland 2025). Every other muscle (traps, forearms, front delts, lower back, obliques, adductors, abductors, and serratus, rotator cuff, neck and tibialis, tracked only since r29) is counted and shown with no target until the lifter gives it one (Set a target, a focus, or a target typed before r58 on one of the seven: `hasTargetIn`), and then it counts everywhere a target counts. Before r58 the seven had default targets no study sets, which read almost every routine as short, and the four's boxes in Weekly set targets did nothing.
- **First-run targets (a convention, r29):** picking a ready-made routine on a first run lowers each weekly minimum to what it plans, only while every target is still the default and never raising anything; targets the lifter set are left alone. It moves no number in the evidence; it says the plan is the lifter's target, and the toast says so.
- **Second review conventions (r31):** the working load is the load done for the most sets among loads at 60% or more of the session's heaviest; flat is under 0.1% a week; a new level is 3 or more lighter sessions in a row over 2 weeks or more; a trend needs 3 sessions over 2 weeks; load-finding reads sets past 12 reps to failure with Epley up to 30 (never shown) and allows a 25% jump in a lift's first 3 sessions; only compound lifts with an estimate count toward a deload suggestion; a routine made from an import uses the common ranges nearest the usual reps (1-3, 3-5, 6-8, 8-10, 10-12, 12-15, 15-20). None moves a number in the evidence above.
- **Third review conventions (r32):** a Lighter day or deload is 90% of the normal day's load (or last time's, if lower); a step is large when the estimate says it costs 1.5 reps or more, and then reps build first, up to a quarter of the top of the range past it, 2 at least; one set under the range among the others counts as the bottom of the range; four sessions in a row under the range at one load drop it; a fixed-rep plan (range under 3 reps wide) steps up whatever the step costs; a stall's slope is the median of pairwise slopes; a muscle's trend counts lifts with 4 sessions over 3 weeks; a PR is counted when the set is stronger than every earlier one by the rep-PR score; a dumbbell or kettlebell step is the largest common rack spacing the logged loads fit. None moves a number in the evidence above.
- **RIR 4 to 5 as half a set (a convention, r40, a setting off by default):** growth rises as sets end closer to failure (Robinson 2024), so sets further away still do something; half is a convention, not a measured dose. It moves weekly sets and targets only.
- **Lighter weeks and the first week (conventions, r53 and r54):** a session is lighter when half or more of its lifts were done lighter, and a week when more than half its sessions were or it ends with two or more deload sessions done lighter; a rest week and a week with nothing trained count with them for holding a deload suggestion back. The log's first week is left out of the weekly averages, since loads are found that week with sets ended well short of failure. Neither moves a number in the evidence above; they decide which weeks the averages read.
- **Sessions a week on a cycle (V's call, r54):** a cycle that is not a week long plans the whole number of sessions under its rate (every other day, 3.5, is 3), at least one, for the ring, the streak and adherence. A convention about counting, not training.
- **One rule for every session (V's rule, r54; conventions in its numbers):** a lift sets its next target only at its usual load and reps for its usual sets. Its usual is the middle of this session and the two before it in the range since its last break, leaving out sessions done lighter (by sets, working load and strength reading; before only, for a session labelled lighter or a deload); its usual sets are never more than the plan asks, and in a session on a day of the routine they are the sets its plan gave the lift that day, saved with the session (`ps`), once a session of the lift since its last break did that many (339 h, r58), so fewer sets than planned never become the usual there; each session is read by its own plan, so a plan changed later never rereads the sessions before it (from the review of r58), and sessions saved before r58 read their own sets; a Freestyle session, or one outside the routine, reads its own. A session's sets are its working sets within a tenth of its working load either way (r30's lighter threshold) and any heavier one at its usual working load, a set tried and missed at those loads among them (r64: a missed set is a set short of the range, never a set not done), so a last set tried a step up, or the rest lowered after a hard first set, count, and a back-off set a tenth lighter does not (from the independent review of r54); a session is compared with its usual over as many of its sets at the working load as the usual sets, so a set past them never counts against it. A session with fewer sets, or a reading under 97% of its usual (r30's tolerance), or with no reading a lighter working load or under 97% of its usual reps a set, is short of it: the target repeats, and the trend, stalls and PRs still read it. A break is more than 28 days between two sessions of a lift, the back-after-a-break number below; it restarts the lift's usual, its lighter reading, its trend and its stall. None moves a number in the evidence above: they decide which sessions set targets.
- **A change of units (a convention, r54):** a load typed in one unit is read as an earlier load typed in the other when it lies above that load by no more than half the largest spacing loads come in that its own number is a whole multiple of (5 or 2.5 lb; 2.5, 2, 1 or 0.5 kg): the nearest load over it on that spacing. Only ever lighter, so it takes away a PR made of rounding and makes none. A spacing finer than the load shows (70 kg on 1 kg plates) can read one step past the nearest as the same load. It decides only which sets are PRs.
- **r55 conventions (V's answers of 2026-10-19 and the journey audit of r54):** a set ticked with 0 reps is a missed set, kept and counted toward nothing (V's call); since r56.2 the next target reads one at the working load as a set short of the range, so sets missed never step a load up (the audit: a 5 x 5 with two sets missed twice asked for the next load). A load asks before it is logged when it is more than three times, or under a third of, the heaviest set ticked in the session, or more than twice the lift's heaviest logged (`ODD_SESSION`, `ODD_BEST`; V chose to ask, the numbers are conventions set to catch a slipped digit or the other unit: an unmarked ramp of warm-ups climbs less than threefold a set, a back-off set is rarely under half the top set, and no lift doubles its best in a session). On a bodyweight lift, added load of 90% or more of the bodyweight, or more than twice the most added before, asks the same way (`ODD_ADDED_BW`, `ODD_ADDED_BEST`; V's decision 2, r64; conventions). A load under the empty bar of a lift on the full bar typed as the total asks the same way (r60, V), the bar being the lifter's own (Settings, or the exercise's), so no number of the app's. A rep target counts a set past the top of the range as the top, so it never asks for reps the range does not hold. A timed hold back after more than 28 days holds about 90% as long (`LIGHT_SHARE`, the same 90% as a loaded lift's, r34.2). The weeks counted (averages, adherence, the coach's missed weeks) start after a break of more than 28 days, as a lift's readings do (`BACK_DAYS`, Bosquet 2013, below). A lift in more than one rep range reads falling only when it falls in each (V, PENDING 319). None moves a number in the evidence above.
- **Bodyweight share:** only the push-up figure is measured (64%, Ebben 2011); the rest are segment-mass estimates.
- **Numbers with no study behind them (conventions, labelled in r56.2 from the audit):** a drop set counts half a set (`setCredit`); + Drop starts at about 75% of the set before it; the warm-up ramp is 40, 60, 75 and 85% of the working load; at Finish an unmarked lighter set before the first top set is read as a warm-up when it was logged at 5+ RIR, or with no RIR 5+ reps short of what the best estimate allows at that load, or with neither under 75% of the top load (80% on isolation work); the weekly minimums of the smaller muscles (traps, forearms, obliques, abductors and the like) are set by eye from the major-muscle bands, not by a study; PRs are judged in bands of reps to failure of 1 to 5, 6 to 10 and 11 to 12 (`rtfBand`); the coach reads sessions made a week, rounded to a whole session, against the sessions the plan has before it reads how sets were done (r56.2).
- **Back after a break (a convention, r34.2):** a lift more than 28 days off starts at about 90% of its last working load with 2 or more reps in reserve: strength falls the longer training stops, more in people over 65 and in the untrained than in recreational athletes (Bosquet 2013, a meta-analysis of training cessation: a dose-response with the length of the stop). The abstract gives no number of weeks; the 4 weeks (28 days) is the app's convention for where a break begins, not a figure from the study (the full text was not checked, r64). 90% is the same lighter-day number as r32, not a new one. Every set at 5 or more RIR steps up at least once, since 5 is the top of the scale and only a floor for the estimate. From there the way back is V's decision 1 of 2026-10-27 (r64, Loads going up below): until the lift is back at its load from before the break, each full session's next load is what its own estimate gives for the lift's usual reps at the planned reserve, never below the current load and never past the load from before; back at it in fewer than the planned sets (the next sets moved up within the session), the next session asks every planned set at it for the fewest reps done there (V, 2026-10-27, NEW-5); then the usual rules. Bosquet 2013 supports starting near 90%; no study gives a per-session rate of return for trained lifters, so the climb is a convention.
- **Loads going up, and the way back after a break (conventions, r60; V's rules of 2026-10-24, PENDING 341 b):** (1) a load the next sets or the next session asks is the lift's own estimate's (Brzycki or Epley, above), in whole steps on its rack or plates, never the trend line, which moves with the 2 to 8% day-to-day noise (Sigvaldsen 2023); (2) at the top of the range a lift adds one step of about 2.5% (compound) or 5% (isolation), double progression as before; (3) the 10% cap on a session's adjustment (15% on a light isolation lift, `jumpCap`) applies only past the loads the lift has done since its last break, so a re-approach to a load already done is bounded by the estimate alone; (4) on the first session back after a break the next sets aim where the return's words do, the bottom of the range with 2 or more in reserve, never past the load from before the break, and any set in the range whose estimate gives a heavier load moves them, not only one clearly off the plan, a blank effort read as 2 in reserve (V's decision 1, r64: 165 x 10 at 2 in reserve, or unrated, gives 185 x 6, as 165 x 10 at 3 does; 165 x 6 at 3 gives 170; 165 x 6 at 2 leaves them at 165); (5) until a lift is back at its load from before the break, each full session done as asked (every set at its load reaching the bottom of the range) sets the next load at what that session's own estimate gives for the reps the lift was done for before the break at the planned reserve, in whole steps on its rack or plates, never below the current load and never past the load from before the break; once a session since the break is done at that load, the usual rules alone; a blank effort on the first session back reads as 2 in reserve, what it asked, everywhere it sets a load (V's decision 1 of 2026-10-27, replacing r60's rule 5, which read the session after the return only: a full-strength lifter, 185 x 8, 8, 7 at 1 on 6-10 before six weeks off, who does 165 x 10 x 3 at 2, then 175 x 9 x 3 at 1, then 180 x 9 x 3 at 1, is asked 175, 180 and 185; until r64, 175, 175 and 180, and from 165 x 6 x 3 as asked the climb stopped at 175 x 9 for weeks). No source gives a per-session rate of return for trained lifters (the retraining studies found are in older or untrained adults), so the way back is estimate-led and capped by the load from before the break; the numbers are conventions. `tests/loadrules.js` holds them across lifts, units, racks and breaks.
- **Your pace (a convention, r43):** the time model (40 s a set and so on) is itself a convention; once 3 sessions are timed, estimates scale by the median of how long yours took against it, over the newest 8 in 12 weeks, leaving out a third or three times the model as a clock left on or stopped. It moves minutes only, never a load, a rep or a set.
- **Your training (r44):** heavier loads gave more strength for similar growth (Schoenfeld 2017, meta-analysis of low vs high loads; ACSM 2009 position stand, 1 to 6 RM for strength past the novice stage, 8 to 12 RM for novices), and 3 minutes' rest beat 1 for strength and size (Schoenfeld 2016). 5 to 8 for Both, 2 in reserve on heavy compounds, and 3 or 2 in reserve for a first year are conventions. Focus muscles: more weekly sets grow a muscle more (Pelland 2025); 30% up is a convention; the rest at a third of the minimum is maintenance (Bickel 2011; Spiering 2021 review). The strength focus's 5 days and 3% are conventions (3% is the app's own threshold for a real change).
- **Waist-to-height:** 0.5 / 0.6 bands (NICE 2022).
- **Tape measurements:** changes under about 1 cm are treated as noise.
- **Long-length bonus:** only for exercises backed by trials (seated leg curl, overhead triceps, standing calf raise) or by mechanics (labelled as such).

## Where each number lives (r61)

Since r61 every number a rule uses is a named constant in `src/app/`, declared once at the top of its module and read by the rule and by the words that state it (PENDING 350; `tests/hygiene.js` fails on any other number past 0, 1 and 2). This list goes from each constant to its source and back. Left out, since no lifter's numbers depend on them: pixel sizes, chart styling, animation and toast timings, ids, storage keys, version numbers, labels and choice lists with no rule in them, the copy built from these constants, the ready-made routines' tables (data, not policy: PENDING 350), the import reader's formats (dates, zip files, column names), and tolerances that only absorb rounding (`PLATE_TOL_MIN`, `LOAD_KEY`, `PLATE_DUST`). "Above" names the paragraph of this document that discusses the number.

### Loads and progression

- `JUMP_COMPOUND` 0.025 and `JUMP_ISOLATION` 0.05 (formulas): the load added at the top of the range when the plan sets no jump. A convention, double progression (r30; Loads going up, above).
- `JUMP_CAP` 1.1, `JUMP_CAP_LIGHT` 1.15 and `LIGHT_LIFT_KG` 20 (formulas): the most one session's adjustment may raise a load, 15% on an isolation lift under 20 kg. Conventions, in the app since r13 (Well past the range, and Loads going up, above).
- `JUMP_CAP_EARLY` 1.25 and `EARLY_SESSIONS` 3 (formulas): the cap in a lift's first three sessions, while its load is found, counted since its last break or New machine or gym, in any rep range (r64; until r64 the sessions in the plan's range only, so a range changed on a long-trained lift was allowed 25%). A convention (r31; Second review conventions, above).
- `CLEARLY_OFF` 2 (formulas): the reps or reps in reserve off the plan that move the next session's load and the next sets. A convention (r27, r34; 3 until r45, V).
- `FATIGUE_RIR` 1 (formulas): the rep more in reserve a load worked out from an estimate leaves, in the next sets and a close variation's first session. A convention, meant to err light (r32.1, r34).
- `JUST_UP` steps: 1.5, short: 3 (engine): just after a step up of no more than 1.5 steps, reps short by up to 3 are the step's, not a drop. A convention (r31).
- `SHORT_RUN` 4 (engine): sessions in a row under the range at one load that drop the load. A convention (r32; Third review conventions, above).
- `REPS_MAXED` min: 3, share: 0.4 (engine): at the heaviest load available, a load step of 0, or bodyweight never loaded, every set at the top plus 40% of it (3 reps at least) is told to make reps harder. A convention (r27).
- `SLOW_LOWER_S` 3 to 4 (engine): the slower lowering that advice suggests, in seconds. A lifting convention (r27), never read back by the app.
- `FIXED_RANGE` 3 (engine): a range narrower than 3 reps is a fixed-rep plan, stepped up whatever the step costs. A convention (r32; Third review conventions, above).
- `BIG_STEP_REPS` 1.5 (engine): a step that costs 1.5 reps or more is large for the load, so reps build first. A convention (r32; Third review conventions, above).
- `BUILD_PAST` min: 2, share: 0.25 (engine): the reps built past the top before a large step, a quarter of the top of the range, 2 at least. A convention (r32; Third review conventions, above).
- `KIN` days: 120, compound: 0.8, isolation: 0.9 (engine): a first session from a close variation trained in the last 120 days, at 80% of its estimate on a compound lift and 90% on an isolation lift. A convention, to err light (r32.1).
- `LIGHT_SHARE` 0.9 (formulas): the share of the normal load a lighter day, a deload, and a lift back after a break ask for, and of the hold on a timed hold back after one. A convention, the usual deload of about a tenth (r32, r55; Third review conventions and Back after a break, above).
- `LIGHTER_SET_SHARE` 1/2 (formulas): the share of a lift's sets a lighter day, a deload, and Make it lighter keep, rounded up. A convention (a deload's half sets since r11, the oldest build kept; the lighter day's since r30).
- `LIGHT_RIR` 3 (format): the reps in reserve a lighter day, a deload, and an injury hold ask for at least. A convention (r30).
- `BACK_DAYS` 28 (formulas): more days than this between two sessions of a lift is a break, which restarts its readings and the weeks counted. A convention: Bosquet 2013 shows strength falling with the length of the stop but names no number of days (Back after a break, above; r34.2, wording r64).
- `BACK_RIR` 2 (formulas): the reps in reserve a lift back after a break keeps at least, and what an unrated set of it reads as. A convention (r34.2; Back after a break, above).
- `ODD_SESSION` 3 and `ODD_BEST` 2 (engine): a load more than three times, or under a third of, the heaviest set ticked today, or more than twice the lift's heaviest logged, is asked about first. Conventions (r55; r55 conventions, above).
- `ODD_ADDED_BW` 0.9 and `ODD_ADDED_BEST` 2 (engine): on a bodyweight lift, added load of 90% or more of the bodyweight, or more than twice the most added on the lift before, is asked about first (V's decision 2 of 2026-10-27, from the r63 audit: bodyweight typed as added load was saved and became a PR). Conventions, to catch the bodyweight typed in the field for added load and a slipped digit; no study sets them (r64).
- `ODD_REPS` 30 and `ODD_REPS_TIMES` 2 (worksets): reps over 30 and more than twice the top of the range and anything logged on the lift are asked about first. A convention, for a slipped digit (r32).
- `MIX_RANGE` [0.6, 1.4] (worksets): a load under 60% or over 140% of last time's is asked about once, a per-dumbbell or per-side mix-up. A convention, release not recorded.
- `DROP_START` 0.75 (formulas): + Drop starts at about 75% of the last working load. A convention (r56.2; Numbers with no study behind them, above).
- `WARM_RAMP` [0.4, 0.6, 0.75, 0.85] (formulas): + Warm-up's loads as shares of the working load. A convention (r56.2; Numbers with no study behind them, above).
- `RACK_FIT` 0.01 and `RACK_READ_N` 12 (engine): a dumbbell or kettlebell rack's spacing is read from the lift's newest 12 sessions, a load fitting it within 1% of a step. A convention, with r32's rack spacing (Third review conventions, above).
- `OFF_STEP` 0.02 (engine): a load more than 2% of a step from the unit's step is off it (a log kept in the other unit). A convention (r54). Since r64 the unit's steps are counted from the bar on a lift on the full bar typed as the total, and a load rounded onto them up from last time never passes both what was asked and the 10% cap (`jumpCap`) while a step under it is still above last time.
- `BELL_TOL` 0.45 (gym): a load is the bell it is nearer than 45% of the gap to its neighbours. A convention (r34.2).
- `TYPED_STEP` 0.05 (units): what a load typed in its own unit is a whole number of, so a converted one reads as converted. A convention (r54; A change of units, above).
- `BAR` lb: 45, kg: 20 (units): the standard bar in the unit a gym is labelled in, used until the lifter sets their own. The standard bars, not a study.
- `DEFAULT_STEP` kg: 2.5, lb: 5 and `STEP_LB` (units): the load step when none is set, the lightest pair of standard plates. A convention (5 lb read as 2.5 kg in kilograms since r27).
- `PLATES_LB` [45, 35, 25, 10, 5, 2.5] and `PLATES_KG` [25, 20, 15, 10, 5, 2.5, 1.25] (units): the plates counted for a barbell load unless Your gym lists its own. The standard plate sets, not a study.
- `SMALL_PLATE` lb: 1.25, kg: 0.5 (units): the change plate added with Small change plates on. A convention (r29, item 98).
- `LB` 0.45359237, `IN` 2.54, `KM_MI` 1.609344 and `FT_IN` 12 (units): unit conversions, exact by definition.

### Effort and estimates

- `E1_MAX_REPS` 12 (formulas): no estimated 1RM past 12 reps to failure, where formulas spread too far (Nuzzo 2024, Halperin 2022; 1RM, above; since r13).
- `BRZYCKI_A` 36, `BRZYCKI_B` 37 and `BRZYCKI_TOP` 10 (formulas): Brzycki's formula (Brzycki 1993) up to 10 reps to failure, where it meets Epley (1RM, above).
- `EPLEY_REPS` 30 (formulas): Epley's formula (Epley 1985), 11 and 12 reps to failure (1RM, above).
- `E1_MAX_RIR` 4 (formulas): a set called at more than 4 in reserve gives no estimate, and load-finding reads one as 4, which errs low. A convention (r13; r11 read every set as 4 at most).
- `RIR_CALL_TOP` 5 (formulas): the top of the effort strip, read as "this many or more", so only a floor. A convention (r34.2; Back after a break, above).
- `EFFORT_TOP` 10 (records): RIR is stored from 0 to 10, and RPE is 10 less it (RPE 10 is no reps left). The RPE scale's own definition.
